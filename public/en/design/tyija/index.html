Ty i Ja

2024

Ty i Ja [You and Me] was a unique lifestyle magazine published in communist Poland in the 1960s and 1970s. The monthly featured articles on contemporary art and literature, interviews with famous artists, scientists, and intellectuals, guides, and recipes. In 2024, the History Meeting House in Warsaw opened an exhibition about the magazine. Julia Libera and Agata Szydłowska, the curators of the show, told the story of the people who made it – both journalists and graphic designers – in several sections reflecting the magazine’s content, such as fashion, dwelling, cinema, and literature.

It was a real treat and great pleasure for me as a designer to work with the vast Ty i Ja archive while creating the visual communication and the catalogue layout.

Whilst working on this project, I felt as though I were rebranding the magazine. However, the result wasn’t a new page layout, but posters and the visual identity of the exhibition and the book.

My starting point and main inspiration was the second Ty i Ja nameplate with its characteristic letters cropped at the top and bottom. I decided to keep the cropping rule, but apply it to contemporary typefaces. I chose two fonts by Jan Estrada-Osmycki from Tekio: Softcore for headlines and Pretty for body text.

The second Ty i Ja nameplate

Today, a poster doesn’t come in one format: each place where it appears needs its own proportions. I usually just adapt the design to each new format, but here, with such a huge number of graphic gems to draw on, I was determined to create as many different designs as possible.

The vibrant exhibition design was created by Lotne Studio, based on my key visual, which set out the colour palette and typography. The show featured mainly material from Ty i Ja but also historic design tools, video interviews with the magazine’s creators, and much more.

Exhibition photos by Maks Januszewicz / DSH

Composing the catalogue pages was a process of selecting, sorting, and finding the best relationships between fragments and details from the historic source. The research, especially into the visual content, led to plenty of surprising discoveries – like these animations, which I found on one of the spreads and set back in motion.

The catalogue has similar proportions to the magazine, but it is smaller, so that it is easier to handle. A key element is a list of all the original covers, shown on two fold-out pages. The colours and typefaces are consistent with those of the exhibition. Its 180 pages are filled with photographs, original graphic designs, and sophisticated details.

The book received two awards in the Institute of Industrial Design’s competition: Good Design and a special award named after the Institute’s founder, Wanda Telakowska.

In their statement the jury said:

The prize was awarded for an exceptional visual journey to the time of the Polish People’s Republic. The awarded album contains a curated selection of materials from the Ty i Ja monthly – reproductions of original spreads, collages and individual illustrations, and texts explaining the historical and cultural context.
